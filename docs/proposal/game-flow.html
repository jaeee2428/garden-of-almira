The loop

Every page works the same way

One loop, eighteen times. Each chapter adds one new kind of clue, so the player always knows what to do and still meets something new.

1 · Read the page→2 · Collect clues→3 · Fill the blanks→4 · Find the flower→5 · Tell Lola→6 · Hear the poem

Three blanks, always

Ngalan (name), Kanus-a / Asa (when or where), Gamit (use). They are the parts Lola could not see to write.

Words come from clues

Every clue adds words to the word bank. You fill blanks only with words you found. No guessing from a list of 18.

Never stuck

A wrong word just does not stick. A weak description makes Lola ask one question back. No timer, no game over.

What changed from v0.2

Simpler and more direct

  • Her poems leadMa'am Alburo gave permission, so her poem is the reward on every page she wrote about. It is no longer a "stretch" item.
  • One loopRemedy pages are no longer a separate system. In Chapter III a visitor asks for help, and the answer is still a page with three blanks.
  • One new thing per chapterI: riddles. II: neighbours. III: visitors with a need. IV: scent only. Nothing else is added mid chapter.
  • Describe with chips"Tell Lola" is three chips picked from what you noticed. Quick to play, easy to score, easy to test.
  • Fewer keysJ journal, L sit with Lola, hold O for Mata ni Lola. "Tabi" happens by walking slowly past the old tree (no key).
  • Clear ending18 pages done: Ikaw reads the journal to Lola at night, then the existing awakening plays.

Decision · poems

Build around her poems. Do not write new ones.

What we do

Her poems become the heart of the game: the reward on every page she wrote about, read aloud by Lola, shown in Cebuano with an English line under it, and collected in the printable herbarium at the end.

If her work has a poem for a flower that is not in our 18, we add that flower (new plants never reshuffle the garden).

What we do not do

We do not write new poems and put them in her voice or her name. A made up "Alburo poem" would be the one thing a panel or Ma'am Alburo could fairly object to.

Pages with no poem of hers end with Lola's spoken knowledge and the folk use instead. They still feel complete.

What I need from you

The poem text (or a photo of the pages), which flowers it covers, and the exact wording of her permission, so we can credit it right in the game and in the thesis.

Why each choice (RRL)

The research behind the design

Design choiceWhy
The player learns by talking with an elderChildren know fewer plants than their grandparents, and asking them to interview elders started real exchange. (Milani et al., 2025)
Real Cebuano healing plants and usesCebu and Siquijor healers' knowledge is documented and can be checked. (Miano et al., 2011; Siquijor State College, 2015)
Noticing is the core actionPeople tend to overlook plants; the gap covers attention, attitude, knowledge and interest. (Wandersee & Schussler, 1999; Parsley, 2020)
Clue and deduction loopPuzzle and adventure are the most used genres for intangible heritage games. Botany Manor proves the loop works with plants. (Zúñiga-Solórzano et al., 2025; Balloon Studios, 2024)
A story, not a quizHeritage games work best on awareness and feeling, which a story carries. (Mortara et al., 2014; Anderson et al., 2010)
Pre and post quiz plus SUS and GEQGames improve learning when well designed, so we measure it, not assume it. (Clark et al., 2016; Wouters et al., 2013; Hake, 1998; Brooke, 1996; IJsselsteijn et al., 2013)
Cebuano first, made locallyFilipino games with Filipino stories, made for Filipinos, are still rare. (Cerda, 2021)
Consent, credit, no medical adviceTraditional knowledge belongs to its community; folk medicine has its own law. (RA 8371; RA 8423)
Some gaps may be the land, not lost teachingUse care when claiming loss; we say "often" and test our own players. (Hanazaki et al., 2013)
Let's brainstorm

Questions to decide together

  1. Which flowers do her poems cover? If her list differs from our 18, do we follow her list?
  2. Who plays it? Senior high, college, or both? This sets the reading level and the quiz.
  3. Where does Lola sit? The papag by the kubo, the duyan, or a window of the bahay?
  4. Order of flowers. Is the chapter split above right, or should a flower move (for example kalachuchi to the night for kalag-kalag)?
  5. English help. Always on under the Cebuano, or a key to show it?
  6. Voice. Recorded Cebuano voice for Lola, or text only for now?
  7. Who checks the Cebuano and the folk uses? Ma'am Alburo, Ma'am Gabales, a local elder or a mananambal?
